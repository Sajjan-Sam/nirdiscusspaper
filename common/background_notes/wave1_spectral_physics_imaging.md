**Key facts for the student:**
- **IDD-AW camera.** It uses a **JAI FS-3200D-10GE 2-CMOS prism camera**: RGB at 400–670 nm and NIR at 740–1000 nm. Its RGB therefore has **no NIR contamination**, so IDD-AW cannot validate S3. The upside is that S1/S4 can simulate this exact camera (JAI publishes its curves [UV]).
- **Fog physics.** Fog droplets scatter in the Mie regime, which is roughly flat across visible–NIR. Only haze follows λ^-α with α ≈ 0.7. This is consistent with NIR being weak in IDD-AW fog (58 vs 64).

### (3) Ranked top-2

**1. S2 with S5 folded in: one physics-derived quantity used two ways.** Compute camera-conditioned, noise-aware conditional information (PID terms: unique NIR, unique RGB, redundant). Use ICVL-RAW, USGS v7 and the JAI/MCubeS camera curves under daylight and low-light noise. Use the result twice: (i) as a class-level regularizer or gating prior in an RGB-NIR fusion network, and (ii) to define Mondrian/hierarchical conformal strata, so prediction sets shrink exactly where NIR breaks metamerism. The paper's central claim is falsifiable: predicted complementarity rank-correlates with the measured per-class fusion gain on IDD-AW and MCubeS. Closest work stops short of this. Shah et al. (2508.10962) select bands on hyperspectral data and never touch fusion training or calibration. Salamati and Prasad are empirical or channel-design studies. Liang's PID is generic and not physics-based. Evaluation is on public real data (mIoU, Safe mIoU, coverage/set size), so "beat SOTA" claims are checkable. It fits one GPU (SegFormer/CMNeXt scale) and needs no hardware. The main risk is small mIoU gains, which the coverage/set-size results and the correlation analysis offset. Targets: TIP, TCSVT, Pattern Recognition, Information Fusion.

**2. S4, using S1 as its engine: weather-aware differentiable RGB+NIR sensor co-design.** Build a differentiable simulator that captures the physics that matters:
- haze vs Mie fog scattering
- sun vs 850/940 nm active illumination
- quantum efficiency (QE) roll-off
- per-channel Poisson-Gaussian noise
- the JAI camera curves

Train on H-City V2 (labels, 450–950 nm), with monocular depth for fog. The gap is clear: LQE is clear-weather and has no RGB+1 constraint, and Beyond Bayer is RGB-only. There is also real-data validation without new hardware: (a) **HSI-Drive v2's real foggy/rainy 25-band 600–975 nm images** test the per-band contrast loss the model predicts (per-weather image counts [UV]); (b) IDD-AW tests sim→real pretraining for the broadband band. It fits one GPU with lightweight segmenters. Risks are that the band conclusions stay simulation-only and the Galway group could scoop. This makes it best suited to TCI or TIP.

S3 is open but under-benchmarked and practically needs real captures, so I would treat it, merged into S6a, only as a fallback.
