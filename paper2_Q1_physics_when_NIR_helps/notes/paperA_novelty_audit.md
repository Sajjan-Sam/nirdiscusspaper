# Paper A novelty audit (hostile A* reviewer view): counterfactually-supervised, physics-informed RGB–NIR fusion for segmentation

Searched up to 2026-11-03 with the arXiv API (abstract and full-HTML reads), WebSearch, and the Semantic Scholar citation list of IDD-AW. This extends `wave1_rgbnir_perception_sota.md` and `wave1_datasets_audit.md`; it does not repeat them.

**Headline:** Three arXiv preprints from Jul–Aug 2026 shrink Paper A's novelty a lot:
- TIER-MoE (2607.27289): cross-fitted, out-of-fold (OOF) modality-risk targets for routing.
- Boundary-Aligned Contribution Routing (2608.15261): leave-one-modality-out (LOMO) utility, a "negative-transfer rate" metric, and Spearman correlation between gates and utility.
- GAUGE (2608.05608): counterfactual gating.

All three are sample-level and none is segmentation. The dense, cross-fitted, counterfactual-target combination for RGB-X segmentation is still unclaimed, but each ingredient now has prior art. Both of Paper A's proposed new metrics are already defined in 2608.15261.

Also, the working title collides with arXiv 2610.36672, "Learning Where to Trust: Reliability-Guided Expert Routing and Velocity Gating for 3D Deformable Registration" (Oct 2026). Rename the paper.

## Q1. Does any work supervise multimodal fusion weights or gates with held-out or counterfactual modality-ablation loss differences, at sample or pixel level? (Contribution C1)

### Takeaway
**C1 is partially done.** Each ingredient exists at sample level:
- Cross-fitted OOF modality-loss targets for routing: TIER-MoE, 2026.
- LOMO counterfactual utility on a frozen model, plus a negative-transfer rate and a gate–utility Spearman: 2608.15261, 2026.
- Counterfactual-effect gating: GAUGE, 2026.
- Held-out-estimated modality weights: G-Blend, 2020.
- Predicted-loss fusion weights: QMF, 2023, and Predictive Dynamic Fusion (PDF), 2024.

I found **no** work that uses a dense (per-pixel) counterfactual Δloss, estimated out of fold, as the *training target* of a feature-level fusion gate in semantic segmentation. That narrow combination is the only defensible core claim.

### Cited Findings
**Closest threats (2026, all arXiv-only as far as I found):**
- **TIER-MoE** (arXiv 2607.27289, Jul 2026; biomedical classification; no venue in arXiv metadata). [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27289); method in [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27289).
  - Defines "sample-specific modality reliability as the prediction loss its unimodal predictor is expected to incur … learned from out-of-fold predictions generated by models that were not trained on the corresponding sample".
  - The method section is headed "Cross-Fitted Conditional Modality Risk" and uses "grouped subject-level cross-fitting" to build "label-honest risk targets".
  - It includes an "OOF MetaGate" control that "receives the same cross-fitted evidence … but directly learns sample-specific modality weights". So cross-fitted supervision of fusion weights already exists at sample level.
  - It evaluates the risk predictor with Spearman, ERCE (mean |predicted risk − realized unimodal Brier loss|), High-Loss AUROC and CMRA, and compares against QMF and PDF.
  - **How Paper A differs:** TIER-MoE's target is the *unimodal* predictor's OOF loss (an absolute risk), not the *marginal* Δloss of the fused model with and without NIR. It is sample/subject-level classification on ADNI, PAD-UFES-20 and FPRM, routes MoE experts rather than per-pixel, per-stage feature gates, and has no seed-noise null calibration.
- **Boundary-Aligned Contribution Routing for Robust Optical–SAR Object Detection** (arXiv 2608.15261, Aug 2026). [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15261); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15261).
  - Defines "model-specific leave-one-modality-out (LOMO) task utility" as "the loss reduction obtained by adding modality m to the complementary stream", computed on the same frozen checkpoint. This is the same quantity as Paper A's u(x), at sample level.
  - Defines a **negative-transfer rate (NTR)**: "the frequency at which full fusion underperforms either single-stream counterfactual from the same frozen detector". This is Paper A's "regret rate".
  - Reports "Spearman correlations between the learned routing weights and model-specific LOMO utility range from 0.45 to 0.66" (Table X). This is Paper A's "gate–utility calibration".
  - **Explicitly does not train on utility:** "LOMO utility is an analytical diagnostic rather than the optimization target of the router … The router receives no utility label."
  - Notes that zero-stream counterfactuals on models trained without dropout "are out of distribution and may conflate modality contribution with missing-input fragility", so it evaluates NTR with checkpoints trained with modality dropout.
- **GAUGE: Granularity-Adaptive Counterfactual Gating of Evidence for Incomplete Multimodal Classification** (arXiv 2608.05608, Aug 2026). [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05608).
  - Computes a per-token "exact counterfactual effect" (replacing the token with a reference), approximates it with a first-order Taylor term in one backward pass, and maps the result to continuous gates injected as attention bias.
  - In-sample, prediction-based (uses the ungated predicted class, not the label), classification only. The counterfactual effect *computes* the gate; it is not a supervised target.
- **Motivation paper 2604.10702** is *prostate MRI*, not RGB-X: "Backbone-Conditional Behavior of Modality Gating in Multi-Modal Prostate MRI Segmentation" (arXiv v4, Jun 2026; 5-fold CV, 180 models). [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10702).
  - nnU-Net gates "collapse into a near-static modality prior (across-case SD 0.0033), while Mamba gates retain sample-dependent variation (0.0365, ~11x larger)".
  - "replacing per-sample gates with their training-set mean leaves nnU-Net unchanged but degrades Mamba". Its conclusion: "Learned modality gates do not universally perform per-sample quality routing", i.e. the effect is backbone-conditional.

**Older, peer-reviewed sample- or modality-level prior art:**
- **G-Blend:** "What Makes Training Multi-Modal Classification Networks Hard?" (CVPR 2020, arXiv 1905.12681). [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12681). It sets per-modality loss-blending weights from the overfitting-to-generalization ratio, estimated with held-out data. Modality-level, classification; weights the losses, not a per-input gate. (The held-out/OGR mechanism is per the task brief and known content; I verified only the title and venue.)
- **QMF**, "Provable Dynamic Fusion for Low-Quality Multimodal Data" (ICML 2023, [2306.02050](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02050)), and **PDF**, "Predictive Dynamic Fusion" (ICML 2024, [2406.04802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04802)). Both derive sample-level fusion weights tied to the unimodal loss, in-sample. TIER-MoE uses them as baselines and reports PDF's risk–loss Spearman of 0.309 vs its own 0.421 ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27289)).
- **Wei et al.**, "Enhancing multimodal cooperation via sample-level modality valuation" (CVPR 2024, [2309.06255](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06255)). Sample-level Shapley modality contribution, used to *enhance the low-contributing modality* (resampling), not to supervise a gate. Experiments are classification only: MM-Debiased, Kinetics-Sounds, UCF-101 ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.06255)).
- **Imbalance and gradient-modulation family.** All modality-level or sample-level, classification-centric, and none supervises a per-pixel gate:
  - OGM-GE (CVPR 2022 oral, [2203.15332](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15332)).
  - PMR ([2211.07089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.07089); CVPR 2023 per common knowledge, venue not in arXiv metadata).
  - AGM (ICCV 2023, [2308.07686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.07686)).
  - MMPareto (ICML 2024, [2405.17730](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17730)).
  - Diagnosing & Re-learning (ECCV 2024, [2407.09705](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.09705)).
  - ReconBoost (ICML 2024, [2405.09321](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09321)).
  - MLA (CVPR 2024, [2311.10707](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10707)).
- **Newer Shapley- or contribution-driven training:**
  - Modality-Aware SAM / M-SAM ([2510.24919](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24919)): Shapley picks the dominant modality and the loss landscape is modulated; no gate target.
  - "Contribution-Guided Asymmetric Learning" ([2510.26289](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26289)). The paper calls itself **CAL**, not CGAL. Its contribution metric W^m combines information quantity I(m) and confidence D(m); used for gradient acceleration and an information bottleneck.
  - **ShapKO** ([2607.09884](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09884), Jul 2026): "learns modality-specific knockout probabilities based on validation utility … via Shapley values". Validation-set utility drives *dropout rates*, at modality level.
  - PDMP ([2604.05773](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05773)): ranks unimodal performance to set asymmetric gradient coefficients.
- **Counterfactual supervision of attention (non-multimodal):** Counterfactual Attention Learning (ICCV 2021, [2108.08728](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.08728)) supervises attention through the effect of learned vs counterfactual attention. Reviewers will cite it as "counterfactual attention" precedent.
- **Dense, segmentation-side:**
  - EQUISeg ([2509.24505](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24505)): a self-guided module balances modality contributions; no counterfactual target.
  - RSFNet ([2306.10364](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10364)): "generate the pseudo-labels by saliency detection to supervise the feature learning … confidence gate". Per-pixel gate supervision exists, but the source is saliency, not utility.
  - DynMM, "Dynamic Multimodal Fusion" ([2204.00102](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.00102); MULA workshop at CVPR 2023 per arXiv comment): data-dependent gating, including for RGB-D segmentation, trained with task plus resource loss.
  - **DeferredSeg** (Pattern Recognition, [2604.12411](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12411)): "route each pixel to either the base segmentor or a human expert" with a "pixel-wise surrogate collaboration loss". This is the pixel-level learning-to-defer precedent.
  - MM SAM-adapter ([2509.10408](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.10408)): "selectively incorporating auxiliary modalities only when they contribute", evaluated on RGB-easy/RGB-hard splits of DeLiVER and FMB.
  - BiXFormer ([2506.03675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.03675)): mask-level modality matching.
  - None of these uses a counterfactual or held-out utility target.
- **Dense-utility searches came up empty.** arXiv searches for `"modality dropout" AND gate AND supervis`, `"pixel-wise" AND "modality selection"`, and `"modality dropout" AND "semantic segmentation" AND gate` returned 0 hits. `counterfactual AND modality AND fusion AND segmentation` returned only 2604.10702, CausalDisenSeg (brain-tumour missing MRI, [2604.13409](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.13409)) and an unrelated simulator ([arXiv API query](https://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=abs:counterfactual%20AND%20abs:%22modality%22%20AND%20abs:fusion%20AND%20abs:segmentation)). A WebSearch for per-pixel correctness-supervised RGB-T gates found only RSFNet (saliency pseudo-labels) and GatedFusion-Net (per-pixel gates learned from the segmentation loss, no utility labels) ([Massey repository](https://mro.massey.ac.nz/items/6bd96ca5-e694-4bfd-bf36-41bc00d3fca5/full)).

### Inferences
- **Remaining novelty is narrow:** (i) *dense* per-pixel targets; (ii) the *marginal counterfactual* Δloss of one dropout-trained fused model, rather than unimodal OOF risk (TIER-MoE) or Shapley over subsets (Wei'24); (iii) used as the *training target* of a per-stage feature gate inside an RGB-X segmenter, where 2608.15261 uses it only for analysis and GAUGE only at inference; (iv) seed-noise null calibration of the targets (not found anywhere).
- **Strongest framing:** 2608.15261 is the natural "previous step". It showed learned routing weights correlate only moderately with LOMO utility (ρ = 0.45–0.66) and declined to use utility as a target. Paper A asks what happens when you do, and why that needs cross-fitting: in-sample Δloss on training pixels is near zero because the training loss is near zero.
- **Gate-collapse motivation is weak as cited.** The only evidence (2604.10702) is from prostate MRI, and it shows collapse is backbone-dependent: it does not happen on Mamba. Paper A must reproduce the "replace gate with training-set mean" test on CMNeXt/StitchFusion/FuseAdapt-style RGB-NIR models on IDD-AW. Otherwise a reviewer can say the problem does not exist for transformer RGB-X backbones.
- **Classical precedents a reviewer will also raise:**
  - Mixture-of-experts EM: Jacobs et al. 1991; Jordan & Jacobs 1994. Gate targets are posterior responsibilities ∝ each expert's likelihood, i.e. per-sample loss-derived gate targets. Classic references, not re-fetched.
  - Stacking: Wolpert 1992. Out-of-fold predictions train the combiner.
  Paper A must explain why its target differs: a counterfactual within one shared fused network, at feature level, per pixel.

### Gaps
- I did not read the full text of G-Blend, QMF or PDF in this session (titles and venues verified via the arXiv API). The claims about their mechanisms come from the task brief, TIER-MoE's related work, and prior knowledge.
- TIER-MoE, 2608.15261 and GAUGE show no venue in arXiv metadata. Whether a reviewer will treat them as prior art depends on venue policy (unverified).
- I could not rule out a dense-prediction utility-supervised gate in non-English venues or in RGB-D salient-object-detection "depth-quality" work (e.g., D3Net/DQSD-style quality labels; not searched in depth).

## Q2. Does any work use cross-fitting / double-ML-style held-out estimation inside deep multimodal training?

### Takeaway
**Yes, at sample level:** TIER-MoE (Jul 2026) uses grouped subject-level cross-fitting to produce OOF loss targets for modality routing. G-Blend uses held-out data to set modality weights. Stacking and DML are the statistical ancestors. **Do not claim** to be the first to use cross-fitting or held-out estimation in multimodal fusion. Claim it only for dense, pixel-level counterfactual targets in segmentation.

### Cited Findings
- **TIER-MoE:** "For each subject, the risk target is the loss of an out-of-fold unimodal prediction generated by a model trained on the remaining subjects", via "grouped subject-level cross-fitting" — [arXiv 2607.27289 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27289).
- **Double/Debiased Machine Learning** (Chernozhukov et al.; Econometrics Journal 2018 per common knowledge) — [arXiv 1608.00060](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.00060).
- **Held-out training of error/confidence predictors in segmentation:**
  - MetaSeg: meta-classification/regression of segment-level errors ([arXiv 1811.00648](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00648)).
  - ConfidNet: true-class-probability regression for failure prediction (NeurIPS 2019, [1910.04851](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.04851)).
  - Learning Loss for Active Learning: predicts per-sample loss (CVPR 2019 oral, [1905.03677](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.03677)).
  - These are the "loss-predictor head" precedents for Paper A's utility head.
- **arXiv searches returned no deep-vision fusion work using cross-fitting:**
  - `"cross-fitting" AND multimodal`: 4 hits, all clinical or causal-inference papers, e.g. 2610.30220 (doubly robust mediation with learned multimodal representations).
  - `"out-of-fold" AND fusion`: 14 hits; only TIER-MoE is a deep fusion-gating method.
  - Source: [arXiv API](https://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=abs:%22cross-fitting%22%20AND%20abs:multimodal).
- **ShapKO** uses *validation* utility (Shapley over modality subsets) to adapt dropout rates, so held-out utility already drives training dynamics — [arXiv 2607.09884](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09884).

### Inferences
- The defensible statement is: "cross-fitting is necessary for **dense** counterfactual targets because training pixels are fit almost perfectly, making in-sample Δloss degenerate". Show this with a histogram of in-sample vs OOF u(x). Cite TIER-MoE as the sample-level analogue and DML/stacking as the statistical basis.
- **Reviewer attack:** K fold models amount to a K-model ensemble, so gains may come from implicit distillation of fold models. A distillation-matched control is mandatory (see Q8).

### Gaps
- I did not verify whether any segmentation or panoptic paper uses OOF pseudo-labels (cross-pseudo-labeling) for *gate* supervision. Cross-validation pseudo-labeling is common in semi-supervised segmentation, but I found none applied to gates.

## Q3. Does physics-informed RGB–NIR or RGB–T fusion gating for perception already use per-band noise, attenuation or contrast physics as the gate signal? (Contribution C2)

### Takeaway
**C2 is partially done.** Label-free, hand-crafted reliability signals steering fusion gates are well established:
- Measurement entropy: Seeing Through Fog, CVPR 2020.
- Fourier "spectral reliability descriptors": 2606.01173, 2026.
- Illumination/darkness factors: illumination-aware RGB-T detectors.
- Weather condition tokens: CAFuser, RA-L 2025.
- Atmospheric-scattering transmission estimates: AWFusion, for image fusion, not segmentation gating.

I found **no** perception-fusion work that feeds per-band Poisson–Gaussian noise, per-band contrast-to-noise, or wavelength-differential (Ångström β(λ)∝λ^-γ) attenuation into an RGB–NIR segmentation gate. The specific physics is still open; the idea of descriptor-conditioned gating is not.

### Cited Findings
- **Spectral Reliability Descriptors** ([arXiv 2606.01173](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01173), May 2026, RGB-IR detection).
  - A "parameter-free, 7-dimensional spectral reliability descriptor — summarizing band energy, amplitude ratio, phase consistency, and cross-modal correlation".
  - It drives Spectral Reliability Fusion gating and expert routing.
  - "Spectral" here means Fourier frequency bands, **not wavelength physics**.
  - Reports "descriptor-aware gating improves mAP50 over content-only adaptive gating" and 95.0% retention under six synthetic degradations on DroneVehicle.
- **Seeing Through Fog** (CVPR 2020, [arXiv 1902.08913](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.08913)): "adaptively fuses features, driven by measurement entropy". The dataset includes gated NIR.
- **CAFuser** (RA-L 2025, [arXiv 2410.10791](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10791)).
  - Builds a Condition Token from RGB, "supervised with a verbo-visual contrastive loss utilizing text prompts" built from MUSES attributes (weather, precipitation, ground, time of day, sky).
  - At inference the token comes from RGB alone ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10791)).
  - Scene-level, metadata-supervised; no per-band physics. Reports 78.2 mIoU on MUSES.
- **InfraNet / QualGate** ([arXiv 2607.03795](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03795)): "learns a task-oriented control signal to suppress unreliable RGB guidance". Learned, not physics. Detection on LLVIP, FLIR, M3FD and DroneVehicle.
- **Learned-only reliability gates for RGB-D/RGB-T segmentation, no physics inputs:**
  - RSGMamba ([arXiv 2604.12319](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12319)): reliability-aware self-gating; 61.1 mIoU on MFNet.
  - CrossWeaver ([arXiv 2604.02948](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02948)): "reliability-aware cross-modal interaction".
- **UTFNet** (IEEE GRSL 2023): evidential, uncertainty-weighted RGB-T fusion — [DOI 10.1109/LGRS.2023.3322452](https://unpaywall.org/10.1109%2FLGRS.2023.3322452).
- **AWFusion**, "All-weather Multi-Modality Image Fusion" ([arXiv 2402.02090](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02090)).
  - Has a "physically-aware clear feature prediction module, inferring variations in light transmission via illumination and reflectance", and builds AWMM-100K (100k pairs: rain, haze, snow).
  - It degrades IR differently from visible: "infrared images exhibit only general contrast and detail reduction without explicit weather cues"; distant scenes get more blur ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.02090)).
  - This is *image fusion*, and the IR in its sources is presumably thermal (not verified).
- **HSVF** (IEEE TMM 2025, [arXiv 2507.03893](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.03893)): VIS–NIR long-range dehazing with a semantic stream, plus a "pixel-aligned visible-infrared haze dataset with semantic labels". It is restoration, not reliability gating for segmentation.
- **Illumination-physics gates in visible-infrared detection:** a "darkness factor" scales a spatial gating map in 2608.10680 ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.10680); detail from a search snippet only, not read). There is also earlier illumination-aware multispectral pedestrian fusion (arXiv 1802.09972, cited in wave1).
- **Noise-aware IR–visible *image* fusion exists:**
  - Deno-IF (NeurIPS 2025, [poster page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/120071)).
  - IAIFNet, with an explicit noise map ([arXiv 2309.14997](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.14997v3)).
  - CMFS (IJCAI 2025, [proceedings](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2025/209)).
  - None feeds noise estimates into a segmentation fusion gate.
- **Searches for the specific physics returned nothing:**
  - `physics … reliability` and `wavelength AND scattering AND fusion AND infrared AND perception` (arXiv API): 0 hits.
  - A WebSearch for physics-guided transmission-map gating plus segmentation found no paper with all elements ([search result list incl. arXiv 2402.02090, 2603.02560](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.02560)).

### Inferences
- **What is safe to claim:** "first to gate RGB–NIR segmentation fusion with *physically derived, per-band* reliability descriptors (Poisson–Gaussian noise, CNR, Ångström differential attenuation), with falsifiable per-condition predictions". You must cite SRD 2606.01173, Seeing Through Fog, CAFuser and AWFusion as descriptor- or physics-conditioned precedents.
- **Fragility on IDD-AW:**
  - Three of its four conditions (rain, fog, snow) are large-droplet Mie scattering with γ≈0, so the differential-attenuation descriptor should be near-uninformative there (wave1 physics note).
  - The physics prior is most testable on low-light (noise/CNR) and haze-like scenes. IDD-AW has no explicit haze class.
  - Present the γ≈0 prediction as a *falsification test* the paper passes, not as a gain source.
- **Reviewer attack:** "These are just extra hand-crafted input channels to the gate."
  - Rebuttal ablation 1: descriptors vs equal-dimension learned content statistics, which is SRD's own ablation design.
  - Rebuttal ablation 2: descriptors with deliberately mis-specified physics (shuffled per-band σ).

### Gaps
- I did not find whether any 2025–2026 Information Fusion / TIP / TCSVT RGB-T paper uses thermal-physics (emissivity or temperature) gates; searches were not exhaustive.
- The 2608.10680 darkness-factor detail is unverified (search snippet only).

## Q4. Label-free / semi-supervised extension of the utility head (Contribution C3)

### Takeaway
**C3 is weak and mostly implied by prior work.** Any learned reliability or utility predictor (QMF, PDF, TIER-MoE's risk estimator, SRD descriptors) already runs at test time without labels. Training the utility head on *unlabeled* RGB-NIR frames is not reported in what I found, but on its own it is a minor extension.

### Cited Findings
- TIER-MoE's risk estimator is trained on OOF targets, then frozen and applied to unlabeled test inputs; its routing needs no labels at inference — [arXiv 2607.27289 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27289).
- SRD's 7-D descriptor is "parameter-free" and label-free by design — [arXiv 2606.01173](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01173).
- Semi-supervised multimodal segmentation with missing-modality robustness already exists: "Missing Modality Robustness in Semi-Supervised Multi-Modal Semantic Segmentation" ([arXiv 2304.10756](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10756); WACV 2024 per [CVF supplemental](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2024/supplemental/Maheshwari_Missing_Modality_Robustness_WACV_2024_supplemental.pdf)). It reports that modality dropout *reduced* RGBD performance for all three base models ([PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.10756)).

### Inferences
- **Fold C3 into C2:** physics descriptors act as label-free proxies, and the utility head distils into them. Do not list C3 as a headline contribution.
- **The dropout risk:** 2304.10756's finding that dropout hurt full-modality RGBD performance is a ready-made objection to Paper A's dropout-trained fold models. Report the full-modality mIoU of dropout vs no-dropout models.
- **Distance from colorization:** do *not* fill unlabeled frames with synthesized NIR (Pix2Next-style). That would reintroduce the generative/translation line and the data-processing-inequality objection.

### Gaps
- No search specifically for "unlabeled utility estimation" or test-time gate adaptation in RGB-X segmentation (beyond wave1's TTA coverage).

## Q5. RGB–NIR fusion on IDD-AW in 2025–2026, and whether anyone exceeds 69.8 mIoU (val)

### Takeaway
**No strong-venue RGB-X fusion method on IDD-AW was found.** The Semantic Scholar citation list (29 citing works as of 2026-11-03) contains only:
- Low-tier RGB–NIR fusion papers: UGF-Net (WcCST 2026) and Color Space Fusion (ICNTE 2026).
- RGB-only adaptation: SegBridge, CVPR 2026 Findings, 69.8 val per wave1.
- One unexamined T-ASE 2026 paper (FDFNet) whose modality use is unknown.

I found no IDD-AW number above 69.8 mIoU (val). An IDD-AW RGB-X fusion benchmark is therefore still a new contribution.

### Cited Findings

  - "Continual Adaptation of Vision Foundational Models for Semantic Segmentation in Adverse Weather" (CVPR 2026 Findings, [DOI 10.1109/CVPRF71909.2026.00738](https://doi.org/10.1109/CVPRF71909.2026.00738)). RGB-only; 69.8 val per wave1, not re-verified here.

## Q6. Does an existing corruption benchmark or metric make C4 (IDD-AW-C) or the new metrics non-novel?

### Takeaway
**Corruption suites for RGB-X segmentation exist, but none applies physically correct per-band (wavelength-dependent) RGB–NIR degradations to driving data.** The closest are:
- The sensor-failure benchmark (CVPRW 2025): missing modalities plus Gaussian/salt-and-pepper noise on DELIVER.
- DELIVER's synthetic sensor failures.
- Bishoff 2023: overhead RGB-NIR with physically motivated perturbations.
- AWMM-100K: per-modality weather for IR–visible image fusion.

IDD-AW-C is novel only as a "physically correct per-band RGB–NIR driving suite". On its own it is infrastructure-level novelty.

**Both proposed metrics are taken:**
- "Regret rate" = NTR (2608.15261).
- "Gate–utility rank correlation" = 2608.15261's Table X Spearman, and TIER-MoE's risk–loss Spearman.

### Cited Findings
- **Sensor-failure benchmark** (CVPR 2025 Workshop oral, [arXiv 2503.18445](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18445)).
  - Defines Entire-Missing, Random-Missing and Noisy Modality scenarios, with four mIoU metrics, and claims "the first dedicated benchmark for MMSS robustness".
  - Its noisy-modality corruptions are generic: "2 common noises: Gaussian noise and salt-and-pepper noise", validated on the RGB-Depth-Event-LiDAR combination ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.18445)).
- **DELIVER/CMNeXt** (CVPR 2023, [arXiv 2303.01480](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01480)): "four modalities, four adverse weather conditions, and five sensor failure modes" (CARLA synthetic) ([ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.01480)).
- **Bishoff et al.**, "Quantifying the robustness of deep multispectral segmentation models against natural perturbations and data poisoning" ([arXiv 2305.11347](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11347)): overhead RGB+NIR segmentation with "realistic perturbations designed with physical world conditions in mind".
- **AWFusion / AWMM-100K** ([arXiv 2402.02090](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.02090)): a modality-specific weather degradation strategy for IR–visible fusion.
- **2608.15261** defines NTR and gate–utility Spearman; **TIER-MoE** reports Spearman, ERCE and High-Loss AUROC between predicted risk and realized loss ([2608.15261 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15261); [2607.27289 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27289)).
- A WebSearch found no standardized per-modality corruption suite for RGB-T/RGB-D segmentation beyond the above ([result: 2503.18445 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.18445v3)).

### Inferences
- **Reframe the metrics:**
  - Present a *pixel-level* NTR as a dense extension of 2608.15261's NTR.
  - Present *calibration against cross-fitted utility on held-out data* as a protocol, not a new metric.
  - A useful new twist: a **null-calibrated** NTR that counts only pixels whose regret exceeds seed noise.
- **What IDD-AW-C needs to be credible as A* material:**
  - Validation against real conditions, e.g. rank correlation between model robustness on IDD-AW-C fog/low-light and on real IDD-AW fog/low-light.
  - Physically correct per-band parameters: γ per condition, Poisson–Gaussian noise calibrated from IDD-AW dark regions.

### Gaps
- I did not verify MUSES's (ECCV 2024) robustness protocol in detail, nor whether "MCubeS-C"-style suites exist in non-arXiv venues.
